THE PIECES OF A LIFE: MARK TULLOCH'S BOOKS, MUSIC AND WORK IN BALI

Original feature draft | Prepared for editorial consideration | October 2026

At first glance, Mark Tulloch's work can look like several different careers. There are books, commercially released albums, a Bali bar and a business that builds practical AI and operating systems. He sees a common thread: the experience of living under pressure, finding language for it and trying to build something useful from what he has learned.

Tulloch is Australian-born, originally from Narooma on the New South Wales coast. More than 35 years in hospitality and operations have taken him through Australia, the United Kingdom and Indonesia. His background includes a former role as chief operating officer of Bali Realty. Today he operates Sunshooters Bar & Grill in Kerobokan, Bali, and is the founder of Lumindra.

That professional biography tells only part of the story. The personal material is found in his books and in the music he creates as Conscious Systems. The connection between them is the centre of his public work: one life, expressed in several forms, with each offering a different way for people to recognise something of their own experience.

The Pub, The Punch and The Path begins in the world that shaped him. It moves through the demands of hospitality, the readiness for confrontation and the identity that can grow around being the person expected to handle trouble. Its movement is towards a different question: what happens when the habits that helped you survive begin to limit the life you want?

Bipolar Aware approaches identity through Tulloch's personal experience of bipolar disorder. It explores awareness, patterns and the language he uses to understand his own life. It is a lived-experience account, rather than a clinical treatment guide. His forthcoming third book, Still Here, is intended to continue the connected journey through collapse, presence and rebuilding. Publication details for that book remain to be announced.

Tulloch identifies a hospitalisation in September 2024, which he describes as nearly fatal, as a turning point. It is a part of his story that gives context to the work that followed. His writing is concerned with what happens after a person has to reconsider the roles and assumptions that once held their life together.

Conscious Systems carries those themes into music. His two albums, Systems of Light and Your Inner Voice, are available on major listening platforms. The words, he says, come from the journey he has lived. Songs such as The Man Who Wouldn't Fall give that story a shorter, more immediate form: something a listener can return to without first needing to explain what they are going through.

He has a particular audience in mind in men who have spent years carrying responsibility but find it difficult to talk about the pressure. The invitation is simple: read a chapter, listen to a song and see whether it speaks to you. The work is intended to start a conversation, without requiring the reader or listener to share every part of his experience.

Lumindra is where that same concern with clarity meets his operating background. It connects practical AI, business systems and work with people navigating decisions. Sunshooters provides a real setting for that work, with staff, customers and the everyday demands of a trading venue. The business story and the creative story share a question: what can be built from experience?

Tulloch's line for the connection is direct: "I wasn't scattered. I was building the pieces." The next step is making those pieces easier to find. Readers can explore his published books, listeners can discover Conscious Systems, and journalists can follow the full story at Lumindra.co.
